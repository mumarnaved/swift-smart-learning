A-Levels are one of the UK's most recognised academic qualifications, typically taken by students aged sixteen to nineteen once they've finished their GCSEs. Rather than continuing with a broad spread of subjects, students narrow their focus to three or four subjects that align with where they want to go next — whether that's a particular university course or career path. Because A-Level results are recognised internationally, they open doors to leading universities not just in the UK, but around the world.

How A-Levels Work

A-Level study runs across two years. The first year, often called AS Level, builds the foundational understanding a student needs in each subject they've chosen. The second year, A2 Level, builds directly on that foundation with more advanced content and higher expectations of independent thinking. Most students take three or four subjects across both years. Final exams are typically sat in May and June, with results released in mid-August, though a smaller re-sit window runs in October and November for students who need it, with results following in January. Some subjects also include coursework that's assessed alongside the written exams.

Subjects We Support

Each A-Level subject has its own shape and rhythm. Here's a quick look at what a student can expect in the subjects we tutor most.

English (Language & Literature)

English at A-Level blends close language analysis with the study of literary texts — novels, poetry and plays drawn from a range of eras and voices. Students are mostly assessed through essay-based papers that test how well they can read closely, build an argument, and analyse a text's meaning and craft rather than simply summarise it.

Mathematics

A-Level Maths covers Pure Mathematics — algebra, geometry, sequences and trigonometry — alongside Applied Mathematics, which brings in mechanics and statistics. It's usually assessed across three papers of around two hours each, mixing multiple-choice, structured problems, and longer extended-response questions.

Physics

Physics at this level moves through mechanics, electricity and circuits, waves, thermal and nuclear physics, and fields. Exams are typically split across three papers of around two hours each, and alongside the written papers, students build up a practical-skills record from experiments carried out over the course.

Chemistry & Biology

Chemistry covers atomic structure, organic chemistry, and physical and inorganic chemistry, while Biology spans cell biology, genetics, physiology and ecology — each assessed across three papers of around two hours. Both subjects mix structured questions, data-response tasks based on real experimental results, and longer essay-style answers that test depth of understanding.
